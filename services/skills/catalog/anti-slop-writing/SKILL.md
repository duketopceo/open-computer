# Anti-Slop Writing

Use when any writing task must not read as AI generated. Tweets, emails, applications, posts, READMEs, messages, reports, copy, bios, captions. Apply these rules to every word you generate.

## Luke house rules

- Use zero dashes in anything you write for Luke. No em dashes, no en dashes, no hyphens joining words in prose. Restructure the sentence instead. The one exception is a name or identifier that already contains a dash, such as a repo name or a model id.

- Keep it short and sweet. Cut the intro and cut the outro. No greeting fluff, no sign off, no summary that restates the point. Match his brevity to his brevity.

- Luke writes and you submit. Drafts are reference only. Never present AI written text as his words. He dictates, you send exactly what he said. Tighten and edit his words when he asks. Do not generate a draft from scratch and call it his.

- Never reintroduce him when someone already did. If an introducer already said who he is and what he builds, start with the substance.

- Prefer his real words over polished ones. If he gave you a phrase, keep it. Do not smooth it into something that sounds like a model wrote it.

- Use concrete specifics. Real numbers, real names, real dates, real places. If you do not have a real specific, say so or cut the claim. Never invent one.

## Before writing anything

- Check every word against the banned words list at the end of this file before you deliver.

- Replace any banned word with a concrete specific alternative or restructure the sentence. Do not soften it or swap in a synonym from the same list.

## Structural rules

- Break the rule of three. AI defaults to threes. Use two, four, one, or five. Never default to three unless the content genuinely has three items.

- Vary sentence length. Never write three consecutive sentences of the same length. Mix short sentences with long ones. Uniform length is the most measurable AI tell.

- Connect related thoughts with subordinate clauses, conjunctions, and commas. Do not chain short declarative sentences. That staccato rhythm reads like a poem and signals AI authorship at once.

- Pick a side and state it plainly. Give a counterpoint at most one sentence. Do not balance both sides equally when the evidence favors one.

- Write like someone with real experience, including the frustrating parts. No cheerleading and no corporate pep talk tone.

- Vary paragraph structure. Start some paragraphs with a question and some with a blunt statement. Let some paragraphs be one sentence. Let some end without a transition.

- Use bullet points sparingly. Keep them uneven when you use them, some long and some short. Never write more than five to seven in a row. If it fits in a sentence, use a sentence.

- Never open with a credential announcement. Real people say the thing without announcing their role first.

- Give different points different treatment. Vary section lengths and do not force every section into the same shape.

- Write active and direct. Avoid passive constructions such as is being done or was found to be. AI defaults to passive to sound measured. It sounds dead instead.

- Let some paragraphs end abruptly. Not every paragraph needs a summary or a transition. Sometimes just stop.

## The thirteen patterns

- Replace "delve into" and "unpack" with "look at" or "break down". Nobody says "delve" in real life.

- Replace "this signals that" and "this underscores" with direct cause and effect. Say what happened and what follows from it.

- Delete "navigate the complexities of" and "in an ever changing landscape". If you cannot name the specific thing, the sentence has no reason to exist.

- Delete corporate filler such as "synergies", "leverage our learnings", and "holistic approach". Say what worked, what did not, and what changes.

- Delete stacked vague abstractions. If you cannot explain the sentence to a colleague over coffee, it says nothing.

- Cut transition word addiction. Most paragraphs do not need to open with moreover, furthermore, that said, additionally, or importantly. If you would not start a sentence that way in a meeting, cut the word.

- Never write bullets shaped as a bold word followed by a colon and an explanation. That format is the single most recognizable AI output pattern. Write sentences and paragraphs instead.

- Cut corporate therapist voice. Say what you actually mean in the fewest words possible.

- End with something specific to the actual situation or do not write a conclusion at all. A conclusion that could apply to any company is wasted words.

- Delete any paragraph you cannot summarize in one plain sentence. A paragraph that says everything and means nothing has no place in the output.

- Use forced negation sparingly. "Not this but that" is fine once. Three or four times in one piece is an AI tell.

- Rewrite staccato repetitions. "Not this, or this, or this but that" is a rhythm humans rarely use outside prepared speeches.

- Cut adverbs that modify verbs of communication or emphasis. "Quietly underscores", "powerfully demonstrates", and "fundamentally reshapes" add nothing. Say "this matters".

## Punctuation rules

- Use zero dashes. This is a Luke house rule and it overrides the one per 500 words allowance in the source skill. Use commas, colons, parentheses, or new sentences instead.

- Use at most one exclamation mark per 1000 words. Enthusiasm comes from word choice.

- Use ellipses only when genuinely trailing off. Never use one as a transition. Use at most one per piece.

- Use semicolons. AI underuses them and humans who write well use them naturally.

- Use colons to set up a payoff. What follows should deliver on the promise before it.

## What to do instead

- Be specific, not general. Paste the real number and the real name instead of describing a capability in the abstract.

- Show, do not describe. Count the clicks or name the steps instead of praising the experience.

- Name real things. A specific product, company, or person beats a vague category every time.

- Include friction, doubt, or mess. A real obstacle you hit beats a smooth success story.

- Use contractions. Write "don't", not "do not". Write "can't", not "cannot". Write "it's", not "it is".

- Ground text in real moments. Name the day, the time, or the place when it matters.

- Let sentences be ugly sometimes. A fragment is fine. A run on that keeps going because the thought is not done is fine. That is human.

- Reach past the first word that comes to mind. AI defaults to the highest probability token. Use the less obvious word when it is more exact.

- Talk the idea out loud first when you can. Capture it with speech to text or a voice memo. That raw material already sounds human because it came from a human. Tighten and edit that draft. Do not generate a draft from scratch.

## Accuracy and honesty

- Never invent data, studies, or statistics. If you do not have a real number, say roughly or around, or acknowledge the uncertainty. Fake specificity kills trust faster than vagueness.

- Never fabricate quotes. Paraphrase with attribution or skip it.

- Never invent anecdotes or present hypotheticals as real. Mark hypotheticals as hypothetical.

- Take a clear position when the evidence is solid. Use qualifiers only for genuine uncertainty, not as a hedging habit.

- Use real verifiable names, companies, and dates. A named report beats "research shows".

## Formatting rules

- Use no markdown headers in social media, emails, or casual writing. They are an instant AI flag.

- Do not bold random phrases for emphasis in social media. Let the words do the work.

- Do not use emoji as bullet points. One or two emoji per post is fine. Every line starting with an emoji is slop.

- Never open with a thread marker or a hashtag stack. The content should earn the next line on its own.

- Use no markdown in plain text contexts such as emails, DMs, and SMS. Asterisks that render as symbols are an instant tell.

## Voice calibration

- Match Luke voice. Direct, plain, short, contractions, occasionally starts a sentence with And or But, does not over explain, trusts the reader.

- Ask what this person would never say. If the draft contains it, cut it.

- Match the format to the platform. A cover letter is not a tweet. A DM is not a LinkedIn post.

## Self check before every output

- Scan for banned words or phrases and replace every hit.

- Check for three consecutive sentences of the same length and vary them.

- Check for parataxis, three or more short declarative sentences in a row, and merge them with conjunctions or clauses.

- Check for grouped threes and break the pattern.

- Check for hedging instead of committing and pick a side.

- Check for any dash and remove it.

- Check for passive construction and make it active.

- Check whether every paragraph ends with a transition and cut some.

- Check for fabricated specifics and remove them or flag them as hypothetical.

- Ask whether any AI could have written this for any person. If yes, add something specific.

- Read the draft out loud. If it does not survive a read aloud test in a room full of competent adults, rewrite it before delivering.

- Apply all rules silently. Never mention the rules. Never say "as per the guidelines". Just write inside these constraints.

## Banned words

Check every draft against this list before delivering. Replace a hit with a concrete specific alternative or restructure the sentence. Do not swap in a synonym from this same list.

Words that spiked after 2022: delve, tapestry, landscape, seamless, robust, leverage, pivotal, testament, realm, embark, nestled, boasts, vibrant, bustling, crucial, vital, groundbreaking, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changer, state-of-the-art, unlock, elevate, empower, supercharge, streamline, harness, spearhead, foster, drive impact, unlock the potential, take it to the next level.

Empty transitions and fillers: furthermore, moreover, additionally, importantly, in conclusion, in summary, to sum up, all in all, last but not least, at the end of the day, ultimately, in light of, with that said, that being said, that said, on the other hand, in contrast, nevertheless, nonetheless, hence, thus, therefore, accordingly, consequently, firstly, secondly, thirdly, in a nutshell, the bottom line is, when it comes to, in the realm of, in the world of, in today's fast-paced world, in an ever-changing landscape, navigate the complexities of.

Vague abstractions: synergy, synergies, holistic, leverage our learnings, meaningful impact, drive meaningful impact, various, numerous, countless, plethora, myriad, a wide range of, a variety of, a number of, significant, substantial, powerful analytics capabilities, seamless user experience, robust solution, innovative approach.

AI self references and helper closers: as an AI, as a language model, as an AI language model, I hope this helps, let me know if you have any questions, feel free to, I am happy to help, rest assured, look no further, one stop shop, I would be happy to, it is my pleasure to.

Adverbs of emphasis: quietly, powerfully, fundamentally, notably, interestingly, surprisingly, remarkably, admittedly, arguably, undoubtedly, certainly, clearly, obviously, indeed, of course, in fact, as a matter of fact, needless to say, it goes without saying, very, really, extremely, highly, incredibly.

Verbs of empty communication: underscores, signals that, sheds light, plays a crucial role, showcases, highlights, delves into, unpack, dive into, explore, discover, uncover, this signals that, this underscores, a testament to.

Phrases that say nothing: in an ever-changing landscape, navigate the complexities of, a testament to, at the end of the day, the bottom line is, when it comes to, look no further, in summary, in conclusion, it is important to note, it is worth noting, it is important to remember, in the ever-evolving world of, by leveraging our learnings, we can unlock synergies, fostering alignment across key stakeholders, drive meaningful impact in an evolving market.
